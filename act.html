Most you're willing to lose on one trade, if the stop is hit

You choose this number. The page doesn't suggest one.

How the numbers work
  • Shares = your risk number ÷ (entry − stop), rounded down to whole shares. That's the only formula.
  • If that many shares would cost more than your account size, it's cut to what the account can buy.
  • The loss at the stop and the gain at the target are those shares times the distance. A stop order isn't guaranteed to fill at the stop price.
  • A short entry, a gap or a thin market can fill worse than any number shown here.
  • Where Randy gave no stop, the page can show estimates: your own percent from his entry, and for each firm target the farthest stop that still leaves 2:1 (his rule, which he treats as a maximum). They're marked as estimates, appear only as buttons you tap, and are never filled in for you.

Randy's levels come from the RSOTC feed in your brief. Prices come from Yahoo and can be delayed: check the live quote at Schwab before you place anything.
